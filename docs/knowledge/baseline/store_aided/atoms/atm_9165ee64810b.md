# Czech orthography markers: ě, ř, and diacritic patterns

`atm_9165ee64810b` · **language** / country · cell `u2f2t6k`

Czech uses distinctive letters absent or differently used in neighboring Slavic languages: 'ř' is unique to Czech, and 'ě' appears in words like DĚKUJEME (thank you) and TŘÍDÍTE (you sort). Slovak equivalents differ (DĚKUJEME vs ĎAKUJEME, PAPÍR vs PAPIER), Polish uses ł/sz/cz digraphs, and Hungarian is non-Slavic (PAPÍR/ÜVEG/FÉM). Recycling bin labels and shop signage are a reliable way to distinguish Czech from Slovak, Polish, and Hungarian at a glance.
