# Snow-country vs non-snow-country Honshu roof and road infrastructure

`atm_5abefb204d28` · **architecture** / region · cell `xn22nqj`

Japanese snow-country regions (Tohoku, Hokuriku) show steep-pitched metal roofing, snow-guard bars on roof edges, deep eaves, roadside snow poles, and road-surface de-icing sprinkler nozzles. Hokkaido houses typically use hip-roof metal siding with no clay kawara tile at all. Non-snow-country inland Honshu (Kinki, Tokai) instead uses shallow-pitch clay tile roofs with modest eaves and lacks any snow-fall hardware, making presence/absence of these features a fast regional discriminator within Japan.
