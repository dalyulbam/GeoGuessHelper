# Depopulating Japanese mountain hamlet economic profile

`atm_07fa627a987f` · **economy** / region · cell `xn22nqj`

Many mountain-valley hamlets in Japan's rural fringes (e.g. far-northern Kyoto City wards like Keihoku) combine small-scale wet-rice cultivation with deer/boar exclusion fencing, plastic greenhouse vegetable plots, and conifer forestry with local sawn-timber stacking. Owner-occupied postwar minka on generous plots with maintained garden pines signal modest stable rural wealth rather than poverty, even as ageing populations and outmigration toward nearby major cities leave some shutters and plots visibly untended. A remaining prefectural school and shrine/temple cluster indicates the hamlet still functions as a local service center for surrounding smaller settlements.
