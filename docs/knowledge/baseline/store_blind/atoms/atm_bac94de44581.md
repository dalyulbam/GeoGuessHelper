# Icelandic rural service village economic profile

`atm_bac94de44581` · **economy** / country · cell `ge9h53s`

Small Icelandic coastal service towns (population in the hundreds) often show disproportionately generous public infrastructure relative to size — full regional health clinics, churches, floodlit sports pitches — funded by national redistribution, alongside a thin private economy centered on one hotel (tourism/Ring Road traffic), a filling station, and light construction/agricultural support businesses (concrete batching plants, machinery yards). Vehicle fleets skew new and dominated by crossovers/4x4s reflecting high per-capita income and rural driving demands.
