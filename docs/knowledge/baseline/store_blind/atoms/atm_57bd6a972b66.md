# Icelandic corrugated-metal vernacular housing

`atm_57bd6a972b66` · **architecture** / country · cell `ge9h53s`

Icelandic towns typically lack a historic masonry core; building stock is predominantly low-rise, timber-framed construction clad in corrugated metal ('bárujárn') with bold, saturated roof and wall colors, adapted to wind and treeless subarctic-oceanic conditions. This is a 20th-century vernacular rather than a stone or brick tradition, distinguishing Icelandic townscapes from mainland Scandinavian ones with older timber or masonry cores.
