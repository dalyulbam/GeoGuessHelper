# Beyneu, Mangystau Region, Kazakhstan

보고서: `report_kz_beyneu_46.3143_54.4042_260906_223930_ko-en-fr.html` · 언어 ko, en, fr · 적재 p2
장소 노트: [[v0nxm]]

Beyneu is a fast-growing village (auyl) of roughly 58,000 people in Kazakhstan's remote Mangystau Region, functioning as the country's most populous rural settlement and a key transport, rail, and pipeline junction on the corridor linking Russia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and beyond. Its modern growth stems from 1970s oil discoveries and its strategic position hosting Central Asia–Center gas pipeline infrastructure, the Beineu–Bozoy–Shymkent pipeline, and a major rail/highway crossroads, rather than from manufacturing, agriculture, or tourism draws of its own. Predominantly Kazakh, the population blends rail, pipeline, border-service, and administrative workers in town with pastoral herding families across the surrounding sparsely populated steppe district, while visitors mainly pass through en route to the Ustyurt Plateau's cliffs, salt marshes, and Sufi sites rather than staying for the town itself.
