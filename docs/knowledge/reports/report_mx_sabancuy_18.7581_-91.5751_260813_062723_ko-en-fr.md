# Sabancuy, Campeche, Mexico

보고서: `report_mx_sabancuy_18.7581_-91.5751_260813_062723_ko-en-fr.html` · 언어 ko, en, fr
장소 노트: [[9gqfs]]

Sabancuy is a small lagoon-side fishing town of roughly 6,000–8,000 residents in the Carmen municipality of Campeche, Mexico, where daily life revolves around artisanal Gulf fisheries, a Catholic-rooted community calendar, and an emerging low-key tourism trade. Its economy rests on shrimp, snook, red snapper, octopus, crab and oyster catches, supplemented by beekeeping and a nascent ecotourism/sport-fishing sector centered on its estuary, mangroves, and the Laguna de Términos. Older than nearby Ciudad del Carmen (settled as a Spanish presidio in 1717), it markets itself as an authentic, unspoiled alternative to larger, more developed Gulf coast destinations, with an eye toward future "Pueblo Mágico" status.
