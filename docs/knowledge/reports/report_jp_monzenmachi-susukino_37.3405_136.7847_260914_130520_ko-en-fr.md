# Wajima (Monzenmachi Susukino, Noto Peninsula), Ishikawa Prefecture, Japan

보고서: `report_jp_monzenmachi-susukino_37.3405_136.7847_260914_130520_ko-en-fr.html` · 언어 ko, en, fr · 적재 p2
장소 노트: [[xn9k2]]

Monzenmachi Susukino is a small "temple-gate town" district of Wajima City on Japan's Noto Peninsula, built around Sōjiji Soin, one of the two head temples of Sōtō Zen Buddhism founded in 1321. Its identity blends pilgrimage-based tourism with the wider Wajima economy of fisheries and world-renowned Wajima-nuri lacquerware craft, set amid coastal scenery, terraced rice fields, and centuries-old traditions. Like much of the Noto Peninsula, it is a severely aging and depopulating rural community whose economy and heritage sites were devastated by the January 2024 earthquake and subsequent flooding, leaving it now in a fragile recovery phase.
