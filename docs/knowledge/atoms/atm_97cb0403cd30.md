# United Fruit Company (UFCO) banana enclave towns in Central America's Caribbean lowlands

`atm_97cb0403cd30` · **history** / region · cell `d4bb4ty`

**시기**: 1900 ~ 1980

In the early 20th century, the American-owned United Fruit Company established company towns and rail-linked plantation districts along Central America's Caribbean lowlands (e.g. Bananera district in Morales, Izabal, Guatemala), driving urbanization, railway construction, and export-oriented monoculture. These enclaves gave rise to the 'banana republic' economic pattern: heavy dependence on a single multinational agribusiness employer, company-built infrastructure, and later labor unionization (e.g. SITRABI in Izabal) after UFCO's decline in the 1970s. Successor firms like Del Monte/BANDEGUA often inherited the plantations and remain major regional employers.
