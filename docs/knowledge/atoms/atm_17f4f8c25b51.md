# Poland's most severe shrinking cities: Bytom, Łódź, Wałbrzych

`atm_17f4f8c25b51` · **history** / country · cell `u2vqqje`

Bytom, Łódź, and Wałbrzych are cited as Poland's most severe cases of urban shrinkage (population decline), driven by deindustrialization, natural population decrease, and emigration (notably to Western Europe). Bytom's population fell from a 1987 peak of ~239,800 to about 146,000 in 2024 (over 11% loss in 2019-2024 alone), with vacant tenement rates around 8% in central districts and historically high unemployment (20-25%) — a pattern of demographic collapse, socio-economic polarization, aging housing stock, and mining-induced ground subsidence.
