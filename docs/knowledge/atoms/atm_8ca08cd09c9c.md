# UNESCO colonial-centre façade rules vs unregulated periphery in Andean heritage cities

`atm_8ca08cd09c9c` · **architecture** / city · cell `6s9edy6`

Historic UNESCO World Heritage colonial centres in Andean cities (such as Sucre, Bolivia, known as 'La Ciudad Blanca') typically enforce strict whitewashed-façade and red-tile-roof regulations with colonial balconies, uniformly maintained. In contrast, peripheral and hillside districts of the same city show unregulated construction: exposed/unrendered red brick, unfinished upper floors (incremental self-built housing), and unlandscaped public space, marking a sharp visual and regulatory boundary between heritage core and expanding periphery.
