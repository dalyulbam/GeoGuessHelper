# Bypass effect: highway settlements bypassed by new expressways

`atm_af4bd0b30258` · **economy** / global

When governments build limited-access expressways or bypasses around older highway corridors, roadside hamlets that depended on through-traffic for microenterprise income (snack stalls, tire repair, motels) often collapse economically, a pattern well documented along the original US Route 66 after the Interstate Highway System diverted traffic in the 1960s-1980s, leaving many former highway towns depopulated or reliant on nostalgia tourism.
