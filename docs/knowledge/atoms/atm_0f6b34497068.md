# Midlothian/East Lothian coalfield and its 'super pits'

`atm_0f6b34497068` · **history** / region · cell `gcvy4mw`

The Midlothian Coalfield around Edinburgh's southeastern fringe supported deep coal mining for centuries, peaking at about 3,000 miners in 1948. Monktonhall Colliery, sunk from 1953 with a 930m shaft and producing from the mid-1960s, was one of Scotland's post-war 'super pits' built to supply low-sulphur coal directly by rail to power stations (Cockenzie, Longannet). It was mothballed in 1987, briefly run as a miners' cooperative in 1992, and closed for good in 1997/1998, marking the end of Scotland's last deep coal mines (down from 225 nationalised collieries to zero).
