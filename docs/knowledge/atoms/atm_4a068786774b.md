# Minor Three Kingdoms era states and Jin princely fiefs

`atm_4a068786774b` · **history** / polity

Small polities during and after the Three Kingdoms period included Zhending (254-266), Yan (237-238, ruled by House of Gongsun), and numerous Eastern Wu agnatic princely fiefs held by the House of Sun (from 242) such as Lu, Nanyang, Langya, Qi, Kuaiji, Yuzhang, Runan, Liang, Chen, Huaiyang, Dongping, Chenliu, Zhangling, Chengji, Xuanwei, Zhongshan and Dai. Under the Jin dynasty (266-420) there were also extensive networks of dynastic principalities, both non-agnatic (Chenliu ruled by House of Cao, Chu ruled by House of Huan, Song ruled by House of Liu) and agnatic princedoms of the Jin ruling House of Sima, including Bohai, Chen, Dongguan, Fufeng, Taiyuan, Longxi, Ruyin, Yiyang, Changshan, Beihai, Le'an, Yan, Zhongshan, Dongping, Xiapi, Pei, Fanyang, Jinan, Pingyuan, Qiao, Liang, Langya, Gaoyang, Pengcheng, Qi, Hejian, Anping, Chengyang, Nangong, Runan, Donghai, Guanghan, Liaodong, Shiping, Puyang, Zhao, Rencheng, Xihe, Qinghe, Nanyang, Zhangwu, Xindu, Leping, Donglai, Changle, Guangling, Piling, Chu, Yuzhang, Changsha, Shunyang, Qin, Wu, Wuyi, Han, Dai, Chengdu, Huainan, Zhongqiu, Xiyang, Dong'an, Gaomi, Jiyang, Linhuai, Xiangyang, Nanping, Jingzhao, Guangping, Bacheng, Huailing, Jiyang(Jiyang2), Yidu, Guangyang, Xinye, Zhongdu, Lujiang, Wuling, Ruyang, Nandun, Shangyong, Dongyan, Lecheng, Guangchuan, Jingling, Huarong, Xincai, Sui, Liangcheng, Jiyin, Jin, Yiyang(Yiyang2), Kuaiji, Xinning and Linchuan, dated variously between 266 and 420.
