# Latitude-dependent treeline depression in Arctic and subarctic regions

`atm_7183fc7dcba5` · **nature** / global

Treeline elevation drops toward the poles globally due to declining summer temperatures: in Arctic Norway it sits near 200-300m, while in the Alps it reaches 2000-2400m, in the Canadian Rockies around 2200-2500m at similar latitudes to the Alps, and in Arctic Alaska/Siberia treeline approaches sea level near the Arctic Ocean coast. This latitudinal treeline depression is a well-documented biogeographic pattern tied to growing-season temperature thresholds.
