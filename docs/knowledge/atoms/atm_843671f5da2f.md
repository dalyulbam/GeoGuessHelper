# Anti-Hindi agitations and regional script assertion in South India

`atm_843671f5da2f` · **language** / region · cell `tf346te`

Southern Indian states, especially Tamil Nadu, resisted Hindi imposition through the Anti-Hindi agitations of 1937-40 and 1965, leading to the continued official use of Tamil script and English rather than Devanagari-Hindi bilingual signage. This linguistic pushback preserved the Dravidian language family's distinct scripts (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam) against nationwide Hindi standardization.
