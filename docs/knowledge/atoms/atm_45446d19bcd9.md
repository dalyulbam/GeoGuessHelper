# Kilometer-marker toponyms as 20th-century road-colonization settlements in Mexico

`atm_45446d19bcd9` · **history** / region · cell `9gr3utj`

Small rural settlements along Mexican federal highways in the southeastern lowlands (e.g. Campeche, Tabasco, Quintana Roo) are sometimes named directly after their highway kilometer marker (e.g. 'Kilómetro Treinta y Seis'). Such names typically originate from mid-20th-century state-led colonization and ejido land-grant programs that settled migrant farming families along newly built road corridors, rather than from pre-existing indigenous village names.
