# Ejido strip-parcel field morphology in rainfed Mexican highland agriculture

`atm_b2fcdcfac982` · **economy** / region · cell `9gbn2rp`

Mexican ejido (communal land reform) villages in semi-arid highland zones typically show narrow, radially or linearly aligned rainfed strip parcels bounded by dry-stone (rock-pile) field walls, following valley floors near a river or arroyo. This land-tenure-driven parcel geometry, a legacy of post-Revolution agrarian reform, is visually distinct from larger irrigated commercial plots found in wetter lowland regions like the Bajío.
