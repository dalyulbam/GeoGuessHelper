# Russian/Slavic peasant colonization of the Chüy Valley (late 19th century)

`atm_e20de7719377` · **history** / region · cell `txt5gy4`

**시기**: 1868 ~ 1917

From 1868 onward, Russian and Ukrainian peasant families from provinces such as Penza, Samara, Voronezh and Tambov settled the fertile Chüy Valley of northern Kyrgyzstan under Tsarist agricultural colonization policy. This founded numerous villages with Slavic toponyms (Lebedinovka, Rot-Front, Poltavka, Novo-Pokrovka, Vasilyevka, Petrovka, Romanovka, Novonikolayevka) layered atop the older nomadic Kyrgyz and Silk Road heritage of the valley, producing a mixed Kyrgyz-Slavic cultural and toponymic landscape that persists today.
