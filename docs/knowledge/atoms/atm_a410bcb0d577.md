# Lao-Isan toponymy and vernacular housing distinguish Isan from Central Thai and Khmer-Surin zones

`atm_a410bcb0d577` · **culture** / region · cell `w71dmn8`

Villages in the Lao-Isan cultural sphere of Northeast Thailand typically bear Lao-style toponymic prefixes such as 'Ban' (village) and 'Nong' (pond/wetland), and feature vernacular houses with unpainted hardwood upper storeys raised over open ground-floor living/working space. This contrasts with Khmer-influenced Surin-area villages (toponyms with 'Prasat', 'Ta-', Khmer laterite temple ruins) and Central Thai villages (toponyms with 'Khlong'/'Bang', canal-oriented).
