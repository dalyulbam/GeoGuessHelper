# Łódź as a 19th-century textile industrial boomtown

`atm_85267ed54f12` · **history** / city · cell `u3jy6rm`

Łódź grew rapidly in the 19th century from a small settlement into one of the Russian Empire's (and later Poland's) largest textile manufacturing centers, driven by mass immigration of German, Jewish, and Polish workers and entrepreneurs. Its urban core is organized as a flat, grid-planned industrial city with 4-5 story brick tenement blocks (kamienica) built alongside large factory and warehouse complexes, distinguishing it from hillier or older medieval-core Polish cities like Kraków.
