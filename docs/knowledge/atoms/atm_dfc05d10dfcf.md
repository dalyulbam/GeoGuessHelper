# Resource-to-recreation economic transition in Pacific Northwest gateway towns

`atm_dfc05d10dfcf` · **economy** / region · cell `c2b6twp`

Former forestry/pulp-and-paper industrial towns along the BC coast and Pacific Northwest (e.g. Squamish) have diversified into green-tech, outdoor recreation tech, and sustainable tourism economies as timber industries declined, while retaining legacy deep-water log-boom/mill terminal infrastructure. Such towns often serve as highway 'gateway' nodes between a major metro (e.g. Vancouver) and an alpine resort (e.g. Whistler), driving car-dependent, low-density commuter and tourism growth.
