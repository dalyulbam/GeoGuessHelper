# Redbrick and civic universities as reaction to Oxbridge exclusivity

`atm_766d932ca0da` · **history** / country

**시기**: 1836 ~ 1871

In the 19th and early 20th centuries, Britain's 'redbrick' civic universities—Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Liverpool—arose in industrial cities to provide science- and engineering-focused higher education outside Oxbridge's classics-dominated, religiously restricted, and socially exclusive collegiate system. The University of London, founded 1836, was created partly to admit students regardless of religion, unlike Oxford and Cambridge which required Anglican adherence until the Universities Tests Act of 1871 abolished such restrictions.
