# Angevin kings and Hungarian town-chartering policy

`atm_01813ad314d3` · **history** / polity

King Charles I (Charles Robert) of Hungary, founder of the Angevin dynasty's Hungarian branch, actively promoted the growth of market towns and mining settlements in the early 14th century by granting municipal charters, including to towns in the Kysuce/Žilina region such as Kysucké Nové Mesto in 1325. His policy of chartering towns strengthened royal revenue through market tolls and mining rights while curbing baronial power.
