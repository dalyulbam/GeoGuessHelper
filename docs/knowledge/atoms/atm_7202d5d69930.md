# Erosion-control poplar/pine plantings on New Zealand pastoral hill country

`atm_7202d5d69930` · **economy** / region · cell `rbv6fn6`

In marginal sheep-and-beef hill-country farmland on erodible mudstone terrain (eastern North Island, NZ), poplars and pines are widely planted in pole or shelterbelt form specifically for slope stabilisation and erosion control, alongside supplementary plantation forestry income. This land-use pattern signals low-intensity extensive pastoralism on steep, marginal terrain rather than intensive dairying or arable farming.
