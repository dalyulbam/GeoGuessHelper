# Adriatic post-war coastal holiday vernacular vs. historic stone towns

`atm_3c9007b712ed` · **architecture** / region · cell `srfxgpc`

Along stretches of the Croatian Adriatic coast away from historic Venetian-influenced old towns, housing stock is often pragmatic mid-20th-century-onward construction: rendered concrete-block houses with shallow-pitch orange/red barrel-tile or corrugated fibre-cement roofs, self-built concrete terraces with painted steel railings, and dry-stone/rubble retaining walls. This pattern signals holiday-home and family-apartment-rental expansion rather than historic urban wealth, distinct from stone-built old-town cores with Venetian loggias and campanili found elsewhere on the Adriatic.
