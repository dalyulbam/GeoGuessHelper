# An arrowed town name fixes the axis, not the side — check junction pair, not terrain feel

`atm_5c9530efe534` · **geography** / region · cell `62uxebb`

When a rural Chilean junction sign names a town with an arrow plus two minor destinations, the reliable inference is the numbered corridor, not which side of the town you are on. In enclosed forested Araucanía terrain the north-west (Añilco) and south-west approaches to Villarrica look identical, so inferring a bearing from 'enclosed hills, no volcano visible' is unsafe; rely on the specific minor-destination pair instead.
