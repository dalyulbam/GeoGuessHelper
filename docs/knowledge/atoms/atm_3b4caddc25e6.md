# Czechoslovakism and the suppressed dual-language convergence

`atm_3b4caddc25e6` · **language** / period

Between 1918-1938 (and briefly 1945-1992), the ideology of Czechoslovakism promoted Czech and Slovak as dialects of one 'Czechoslovak' nation, encouraging linguistic convergence and downplaying orthographic distinctiveness. This counter-pattern to independent codification collapsed after the 1993 dissolution of Czechoslovakia, after which both languages diverged further in official use, media, and schooling.
