# Icelandic saga authorship and preservation

`atm_ad2145fa222e` · **culture** / period

**시기**: 1179 ~ 1730

Icelandic sagas were compiled and partly authored in the 13th century, with Snorri Sturluson (1179-1241) as the most prominent named saga writer and chieftain, credited with the Prose Edda and likely Heimskringla. Sagas were preserved through manuscript compilations like the Flateyjarbók and later cataloged by Árni Magnússon, whose manuscript collection anchors the modern Árni Magnússon Institute in Reykjavík.
