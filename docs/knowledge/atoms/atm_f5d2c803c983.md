# Bohemian pond-and-plateau landscape vs Moravian vineyard lowlands

`atm_f5d2c803c983` · **geography** / region · cell `u2f2t64`

Southern Bohemia (e.g. around Tábor and Třeboň) is characterized by a rolling 400-500m plateau landscape of consolidated cereal fields, small artificial fishponds (a legacy of medieval/early-modern carp farming), and dark conifer woodlots, with an absence of vineyards or wine-cellar streets. This contrasts with Moravian wine country (e.g. around Mikulov, Znojmo) which features vineyard rows and cellar streets, and with the flatter, more fertile Haná plain in central Moravia known for large-scale arable farming and brick vernacular architecture.
