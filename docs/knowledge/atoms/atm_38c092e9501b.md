# Incremental self-built brick housing in lower-middle-income Andean peripheries

`atm_38c092e9501b` · **culture** / region · cell `6s9edy6`

Peripheral neighborhoods of Andean cities are commonly characterized by incrementally self-financed multi-storey brick housing, where families build and occupy lower floors while leaving upper floors unrendered and unfinished (exposed brick, rebar stubs) pending future income. This construction pattern, combined with informal retail such as internet/mobile top-up kiosks doubling as mobile-money agents and minibus-based (rather than formal bus) transit, is a common socioeconomic signature of lower-middle-income urban growth across the Andes.
