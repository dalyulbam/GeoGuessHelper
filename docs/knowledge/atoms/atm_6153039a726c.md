# Religious commemoration of natural disasters across Catholic Mediterranean cultures

`atm_6153039a726c` · **culture** / global

Beyond Andalusia, Catholic Mediterranean communities commonly memorialize earthquakes, plagues or eruptions through patron-saint festivals: Naples venerates San Gennaro partly for perceived protection from Vesuvius eruptions, and Lisbon's post-1755 earthquake reconstruction under the Marquis of Pombal coexisted with religious processions attributing the disaster to divine will. This reflects a broader pattern of embedding disaster memory into the liturgical/festive calendar rather than secular monuments alone.
