# Malay kampung house: stilted timber construction with wide eaves

`atm_9f241d0453c6` · **architecture** / polity · cell `w29x2w0`

Traditional Malay kampung (village) houses are raised on wooden or concrete piloti-style stilts, with low-pitched gable roofs (often corrugated tin or tile) and wide eaves for rain/heat protection. This vernacular form is common across the Malay-Muslim cultural sphere of Peninsular Malaysia and distinguishes such villages from Chinese shophouse districts (Chinese-character signage, shophouse rows) or Indian plantation quarters (Tamil signage, Hindu temples).
