# Indigenous dual place-naming and reserve/reservation land systems worldwide

`atm_06bd6a770961` · **culture** / global

Bilingual or dual-naming landscapes co-existing with formalized Indigenous land tenure occur across settler-colonial states: Māori place names and Treaty of Waitangi settlements in New Zealand, Aboriginal reserve lands and dual naming (e.g. Uluru/Ayers Rock) in Australia, and U.S. reservation systems with tribal-language place names such as Navajo Nation towns. These reflect a common pattern of colonial administrative overlays atop pre-existing Indigenous territorial and naming systems.
