# Pre-Qin dynastic fiefs / ancient Chinese states (21st century–3rd century BC)

`atm_e04e47f70fa0` · **history** / region

**시기**: -2100 ~ -221

Numerous small hereditary fiefs existed before the Qin unification, mostly ruled by Huaxia noble houses (Ji, Zi, Si, Jiang, Yan, Feng, Man, Cao, Gui) under Shang and Zhou suzerainty: Yue (2032–222 BC, House of Si), Mixu, Dong, Predynastic Shang (to 1675 BC), Guzhu (1600–660 BC), Qi/Henan (16th century–445 BC), Deng (1200–678 BC), Xi (1122–680 BC), E (12th century–863 BC), Quan (12th century–704 BC), Wu (1096–473 BC), Bei (to 1059 BC), Guan (1046–1039 BC), Feng, Jiao, Zhu, Eastern Guo (1046–767 BC), Su, Tan, Huo (1046–661 BC), Shu, Yu (1046–655 BC), Western Guo with Northern and Southern Guo branches, Wen, Ying, Yuan, Lu, Shuliao, Genmou, Xun, Shuyong, Shujiu, Mao, Pan, Dun, Cao (1046–487 BC), Cai (1046–447 BC), Ju (1046–431 BC), Cheng, Xu (1046–375 BC), Ba (1046–316 BC), Teng (1046–297 BC), Zou (1046–281 BC), Yan (1046–222 BC), and Qi state (1046–221 BC) itself split into Jiang Qi (1046–386 BC, House of Jiang) and Tian Qi (386–221 BC, House of Gui), plus Predynastic Zhou (to 1046 BC), Shao, Bi, and the Zhou Demesne.
