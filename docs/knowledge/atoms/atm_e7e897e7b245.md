# Medieval municipal law families of Central Europe

`atm_e7e897e7b245` · **history** / global

Central European towns spread their legal privileges through 'daughter towns' modeled on a small set of mother-city codes: Magdeburg law across Germany, Poland, Silesia, and the Baltic; Lübeck law among Baltic Hanseatic ports; and various Hungarian regional town laws such as Žilina law. These charters standardized hereditary judgeships, market rights, and self-governance, enabling rapid replication of urban institutions across frontier and colonized regions.
