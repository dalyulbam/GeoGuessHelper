# Faroese vs Icelandic orthographic and toponymic distinction

`atm_88ae5884a999` · **language** / polity · cell `ge9h53m`

Both Faroese and Icelandic use the letter ð, but Faroese place-name morphology favors suffixes like '-vegur/-gøta' and 'oy' (island), differing from Icelandic compounds like '-braut' and '-holt'. Faroese terrain is also characterized by far steeper basalt sea cliffs and narrower fjords than Iceland's broader, gentler fjords, allowing the two North Atlantic Nordic nations to be distinguished by combined language and landform cues.
