# Location-of-rule classification and multi-region dynasties

`atm_ef05eb8e8aff` · **history** / global

The list sorts dynasties by territorial extent; classification can be multidimensional, as with the Ayyubid dynasty, described variously as Egyptian, Syrian, Kurdish, Arabized, and Muslim/Islamic/Sunni depending on criteria. Some dynasties appear in multiple sections because they ruled more than one realm or because a single realm spanned multiple regions, such as the House of Windsor (ruling former British Empire territories and 15 present Commonwealth realms) and the Tang dynasty (ruling China proper, Dzungaria, Tarim Basin, Mongolian Plateau, Inner and Outer Manchuria, and parts of Central Asia, the Korean Peninsula, Vietnam, Siberia, and Afghanistan). Regions with long lines of colonized 'local' dynasties, such as the Indian Subcontinent, have separate lists distinguishing indigenous dynasties (e.g. Chola dynasty, Mughal dynasty) from colonial dynasties (e.g. House of Bourbon of French India).
